# Bildfahrplan (time–distance diagram)

`/bf` (index), `/bf/<corridor>?from=<hour>&h=<hours>`

## Data

Built by `vp run netz:build` from the **ÖBB GTFS** of the whole timetable year. Corridors are defined in `packages/netz/src/corridors.ts` by ordered anchor stations: **all ÖBB [Fahrplanbilder](https://www.oebb.at/de/fahrplan/fahrplanbilder)** that are trains in the ÖBB GTFS (61 of 83; private railways such as Mariazellerbahn, Pinzgaubahn, Zillertalbahn, StH/StB lines, cog and cable railways are not in the data), each under its ÖBB number (`/bf/101`), plus a few zugx corridors spanning several Fahrplanbilder (Westbahn, Südbahn, Koralmbahn, Giselabahn, Rheintal, S-Bahn trunk line). Old zugx corridor ids redirect to their Fahrplanbild (`/bf/brenner` → `/bf/300`); the build adds every station served between two anchors (station list taken from a reference day, Tuesday 2026-03-10), places stations by track distance (GTFS `shape_dist_traveled`, haversine fallback), and collects every train of the year that stops at ≥ 2 corridor stations, with its arrival/departure minutes per station. Runs with identical times on different days are stored once with a **running-days bitset** (same encoding as the train index), so any date of the timetable period can be shown. Details: [packages/netz/README.md](../packages/netz/README.md).

Limits: planned timetable (December snapshot; realtime via the live overlay below); dates outside the timetable period show no trains; ÖBB GTFS only (no WESTbahn/GKB/GySEV trains); trains passing a station without stopping get an estimated time there (passing points, below).

**Passing times**: stations a train runs through without stopping (its GTFS shape passes within 300 m) get an estimated time. The running time between its two stops is spread over the stretches in between by the time at the **permitted speed** (VzG data from OpenStreetMap, `packages/vzg`; stations linked by position, within 400 m), capped at the category's top speed (RJX/RJ 230, ICE 250, EC/IC/WB 200, D/NJ/EN/CJX/REX 160, R/S 140 km/h; assumptions). So a train is later in slow sections (Wienerwald, 65–100 km/h) and earlier on fast ones (Tullnerfeld, up to 160). Stretches without VzG data get the average speed of the known ones; no data at all: by distance. No acceleration/braking yet. Speed limits © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

## How it is shown

- **Index** (`/bf`): all corridors (sorted by ÖBB Fahrplanbild number, searchable by number) with main stations, length, stations, trains/day (on the reference Tuesday) and the mix of train categories; filter by corridor or station name, sortable.
- **Diagram**: x = km along the corridor (major stations bold), y = time downwards. Each train is a polyline through its stops (vertical segments = dwell time), coloured by category; steeper = slower. Regional/S-Bahn lines are thinner.
- **Section and direction**: pick the first and last station (`?a=`, `?b=` station ids) to show only part of the route; ⇄ swaps the direction (`?rev=1`, the diagram is mirrored); "Whole route" resets.
- **Date** (`?d=YYYY-MM-DD`, default today): date picker, ‹ › for previous/next day; only trains running on that date are drawn, with the train count.
- **Single / double track** along the top edge of the chart: one thin line = single track, two = double track, nothing = unknown (tooltip names the stretch). Built by `netz:build` (`src/tracks.ts`): for each pair of neighbouring stations the GTFS shape of a train between them is sampled every 100 m and matched to the nearest OSM track (within 50 m) of the sheet's VzG lines; in the Austrian OSM data each track of a double-track line carries `railway:preferred_direction` forward/backward. Needs `.cache/osm-rail.json` (`vp run vzg:extract`), else falls back to the VzG network pieces. Stored per corridor as `tracks = [[km from, km to, 1|2], …]`.
- **Planning** (`?plan=`, buttons below the chart; PLAN.md "Bildfahrplan as a planning tool"); all parameters are kept in the browser via VueUse `useLocalStorage`: `zugx:bf-min-transfer`, `zugx:bf-node-all`, `zugx:bf-node-all-lines`, `zugx:bf-reserve`, `zugx:bf-reserve-all`, collected punctuality `zugx:bf-punct`):
  - **Knoten** (`?plan=knoten&knoten=<station id>`, or click a station name in the chart): Taktknoten check — lines (category + direction + destination) at the station with their most frequent arrival/departure minute, interval and regularity; the quarter hour where most regular lines meet (arriving ≤ 10′ before, departing ≤ 10′ after: Vollknoten :00, Halbknoten :30) and the symmetry minute per category. Transfers: for every arrival in the time window the first connection per direction with the minimum transfer time (default 3′; green ≤ 10′, amber ≤ 20′, red longer) and near misses (departures up to 5′ too early). Source **alle Linien** (default; `localStorage` `zugx:bf-node-all`): also every other train at the station from its HAFAS arrival and departure boards for the time window (planned times, `lib/bf-node-board.ts`) — other operators on the sheet's line (e.g. WESTbahn) get the sheet's direction from their destination/origin (a station of the sheet, or the destination/origin of the sheet's trains), the rest count as **other lines** (own column: first connection per destination, up to 3). No walking times between parts of a station.
  - **Kreuzungen** (`?plan=kreuzung`): where trains of opposite directions meet next to single track. At a station (both there at the same time): the buffer each way — how late the opposite train may be before this one has to wait (red ≤ 2′, amber ≤ 5′); with the live overlay, the expected knock-on delay (+n′). Meetings between two stations of the sheet on single track are flagged (✕): a passing loop without passenger stop (not in the GTFS) or not possible as planned. Chart: single track shaded, crossings as dots in the buffer colour.
  - **Fahrzeitreserve** (`?plan=reserve`): scheduled running time between consecutive stops (median over the day, per category and direction) vs. the minimum — time at the permitted speed (`minRun` in the corridor file: per category and neighbouring station pair from the VzG speeds, capped at the category's top speed) plus accelerating to / braking from the average permitted speed. **Adjustable assumptions** (kept in the browser, `zugx:bf-reserve`): starting acceleration of multiple units (S/R/REX/CJX, default 0.7 m/s²) and locomotive-hauled trains (0.5 m/s²), service braking (0.6 m/s²), standard supplement (Regelzuschlag, 7 %: below = tight, amber) and the threshold for much reserve (25 %, blue); below the minimum (rounded minutes, speed data) shown muted. A guide value: no dwell, gradients or rolling-stock data.
  - **Pünktlichkeit** (`?plan=puenktlichkeit`): where trains lose time — delay gained per stretch between two reported stations and per stop, from the live overlay (observed realtime times only, no forecasts); mean and share of runs losing ≥ 2′. Collected in the browser while live runs (`localStorage` `zugx:bf-punct`, per sheet and day, 7 days) — a sample, not statistics; a shared history would need a backend.
  - **Kapazität** (`?plan=kapazitaet`): blocking times per block section — the Sperrzeitentreppe. Block sections run from main signal to main signal (OSM `railway:signal:main`/`combined` with `railway:signal:direction`; on ETCS Level 2 lines without lineside signals, e.g. the Koralmbahn, the ETCS markers `railway:signal:train_protection=ETCS:marker`; per corridor in `blocks.up/down`, built by `netz:build` from the VzG lines; where a station pair has no signal of a direction, the stations are the block boundaries). Blocking time of a train in a block: from passing the distant signal of the entry signal (approach distance before the block; or its departure, if it stands at a station before the block) minus route setting + sight/reaction time, until its tail has cleared the exit signal (train length) plus release time (`lib/bf-capacity.ts`). Chart: a box per train and block in the category colour, red = conflict (overlap beyond the tolerance in the same block; on single track also opposite trains outside station limits); coupled trains (identical times) count once. Table per block section: trains, occupancy of the time window (union of the blocking times; UIC 406 colours ≤ 60 % / ≤ 75 % / above), longest blocking time (≈ minimum headway), conflicts; bottleneck in the summary; filter "only bottlenecks". Crossing places without passenger stop (`blocks.places`: OSM stations between the timetable's stations, and passing loops recognised by entry and exit signals of both directions within 2 km, e.g. Langwies / Mitterweißenbach on the Salzkammergutbahn) also serve the Kreuzungen panel. **Adjustable** (`zugx:bf-blocks`): approach distance (1000 m), route setting + sight/reaction (0.4′), release (0.1′), train length multiple unit / loco-hauled (150 / 400 m), tolerance (0.5′, timetable minutes are rounded). **Parallel lines** (e.g. Unterinntal: old line 101 04 above ground, new line 330 01 mostly in tunnels): `netz:build` decides per train and station pair from its GTFS shape which VzG line it runs on (samples every 250 m, nearest line within 60 m, ≥ 60 % majority, `packages/netz/src/via.ts`); stretches on another line than the sheet's own (the line of its stations) are stored in `CorridorTrain.via`, that line's block signals in `blocks.alt` (placed by position onto the sheet's km axis). ÖBB shapes often follow the old line even for trains in the tunnel (e.g. IR 515, ICE 1204), so a train without a stop on a stretch that ≥ 5 trains demonstrably run on the other line takes it too — only for bypasses (the other line has no stations of its own there) skipping at least two stations; the build knows the passing points exactly (not stored). Those trains' boxes use the other line's blocks as a separate resource (dashed in the chart, tagged ↳ 330 01 in the table) and don't conflict with trains on the old line. Stretches without any signal data (e.g. Salzburg – Kufstein through Germany) get no boxes. Limits: even speed between stops, no station track occupation; within 4-track stretches of one line number the tracks are not told apart.
- **Timetable tables** below the chart, both directions of the shown section and time window. Default: **Kursbuch layout** like the printed ÖBB Fahrplanbilder — stations as rows ("an"/"ab" rows at the major stations and the ends, grey bands between major stations), a column per train (category and number, coloured by category, linked to the train), "von" / "nach" where the train starts or ends outside the section, a vertical line where it passes without stopping; split into blocks of as many trains as fit the width. **Transpose** (`?transpose=1`): a row per train, stations as columns. Not (yet) from the book: footnotes with running days (the table shows one date), service symbols, connecting trains below the table.
- **Second monitor**: chart and tables can each open in their own window (`?part=chart|table`); sheet, date, time window, section and the hovered train stay in sync (BroadcastChannel).
- **Keyboard** (zapping): ← / → previous / next day, ↑ / ↓ two hours earlier / later (h / l one hour), Shift + ↑ / ↓ a whole window, + / − Maßstab, n = now.
- **Maßstab** (time scale, `?y=`): 2× and 4× stretch the time axis (taller chart, the page scrolls) — for dense timetables; the 10-minute grid appears once there is room.
- Window: scroll (mouse wheel / trackpad) over the diagram to move in time (10 % of the window per notch), Ctrl/⌘ + scroll or pinch to zoom (1–24 h), Shift + scroll stays horizontal; or use the start time with ↑ ↓ and 1/2/4/8/24 h; the dashed red line is the current time (only when today is shown). Category chips hide/show categories.
- Hover highlights a train and shows a tooltip next to the mouse (number, departure station → destination; with Live its delay and last report); click opens its live train run on the selected date.
- **Lines**: after "km · stations" the VzG lines (ÖBB Streckennummern and other railways) through the sheet's stations — found at build time by position (VzG station points within 250 m of ≥ 3 sheet stations) — link to their VzG chart (`/vzg/101-01`). Clicking a station name opens its departure board (Ctrl/⌘-click: new tab); the board links to the station on Wikidata (DinoData `#P954=<id>`).
- **Live overlay** ("Aramis für Arme"; button "Echtzeit"/"Live", `?live=1`, off by default, only for today): realtime paths from ÖBB HAFAS (Scotty) on top of the planned ones (`apps/web/src/lib/bf-live.ts`). No per-train requests:
  - _Past_: one departure board (StationBoard) per major station of the shown section, plus arrival boards at the section's two ends, once per window (≈ 12 requests on the whole Westbahn); each board lists every train there with its actual time. Boards with ≥ 200 trains are paged.
  - _Now_: one radar request (JourneyGeoPos) over the section per minute (paused while the tab is hidden): realtime of the stops around every running train, also at minor stations.
  - _Path_: the GTFS path (incl. passing points) with realtime where known and planned + the last known delay in between (never earlier than the event before). Matching by category + train number (`RJX 162`; HAFAS `s` → `S`). Trains not in the GTFS — WESTbahn, ÖBB trains renumbered since the GTFS snapshot — are drawn through the stations they were seen at, if those span ≥ 3 corridor stations or ≥ 10 km (not e.g. the Südbahn S-Bahn between Wien Hbf and Meidling); their planned path is dotted.
  - _Display_: the planned path of a train with live data becomes thin and faint (trains without live data keep their normal planned line): actual solid up to now, forecast dashed, a dot at every time reported by Scotty (between dots: planned + delay); trains not in the timetable data have a dotted planned path. Current delay at "now" (amber < 5′, red ≥ 5′). A legend below the chart explains the styles; the hover tooltip adds the delay and where the last report came from ("Echtzeit an 3 Bahnhöfen, zuletzt St. Pölten Hbf 16:55").
  - _Limits_: between major stations the past is interpolated (planned + delay), exact minor-station times only while the train was on the radar during the session; trains showing only a line number in HAFAS ("CJX 5") are not matched.
